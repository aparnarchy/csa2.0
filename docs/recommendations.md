## q11 · Compensation

**Do you feel your contributions are recognized by your manager?**

### Employee: current

> Start by making sure your work is visible. Sometimes great work stays hidden because no one knows the full story.
> • Share progress updates in team meetings or after key milestones, so your manager sees the impact you are making.
> • In a 1:1, talk openly about how recognition helps you stay motivated. Be specific about the kind you value most: private feedback, a quick note in a team channel, or public appreciation.
> • Ask for feedback on where you add the most value, so you know where you can amplify it.

<details><summary>Employee: original</summary>

> If you feel your contributions are not recognized, start by making sure your work is visible. Sometimes great work stays hidden because no one knows the full story. Share progress updates in team meetings or summaries after key milestones so your manager sees the impact you are making. In a 1:1, talk openly about how recognition helps you stay motivated. Be specific about the kind of acknowledgment you value most — it could be feedback in private, a quick note in a team channel, or public appreciation. Also, remember that recognition can come in many forms. Ask for feedback on where you add the most value so you understand how your work is perceived and where you can amplify it.

</details>

### Manager: current

> If a team member feels their efforts go unnoticed, be more deliberate with appreciation. Timely, specific feedback often matters most.
> • Review their recent projects and call out concrete examples of where they made a difference.
> • Give feedback in the moment, not just during reviews. Check if they prefer private or group acknowledgment.
> • Build it into your routine: a quick thank-you after a meeting, a message after a milestone, a mention in a team update.
>
> Bottom line: There’s no harm in being a little generous in your appreciation as long as it is genuine.

<details><summary>Manager: original</summary>

> If a team member feels their efforts go unnoticed, it is a signal to be more deliberate with appreciation. Recognition does not have to be elaborate — timely, specific feedback often matters most. Start by reviewing their recent projects and calling out concrete examples of where they made a difference. Give feedback in the moment, not just during reviews. Public praise can be motivating, but also check their preference for private or group acknowledgment. Build recognition into your routine: a quick thank-you at the end of a meeting, a short message after a milestone, or a mention in a team update can go a long way. Make sure they know you see both the big wins and the steady, consistent effort. Bottom line: There’s no harm in being a little generous in your appreciation as long as it is genuine.

### Manager: current

> The standard is set not by your top performers, but by what you and the team are willing to tolerate. If low performance is accepted, it quietly becomes the norm.
> 1. Look at your team’s lowest common denominator: what you currently tolerate.
> 2. Explicitly define high standards: timelines, quality, speed, proactivity. Hold yourself and your team relentlessly accountable.
> 3. Keep rewards aligned. Act promptly when performance falls short, and use regular feedback.

<details><summary>Manager: original</summary>

If low performance is accepted, it quietly becomes the norm. High-performing individuals typically do not thrive in teams where others do not hold themselves to the same standards. This misalignment is often the root cause when teams do not demand excellence from each other. Start by focusing on the lowest common denominator of your team—this is what you and your team currently tolerate. Explicitly define what high standards mean, such as timelines, quality, speed of work, and proactivity. Focus on relentlessly holding yourself and your team accountable to these standards. Ensure that rewards are not misaligned. If performance consistently falls short of the standards, take action promptly, starting from the time of hiring. Use regular feedback to address any missed expectations and maintain alignment throughout.
